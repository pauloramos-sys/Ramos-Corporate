D7 Visa and Buying Property in Portugal: What You Actually Need to Know

You don't need to own property for the D7 visa. Here's when buying makes sense — and when it doesn't, yet.

Ask Paulo Directly

The D7 Visa Doesn't Require You to Buy

One of the most common misconceptions among digital nomads is that you need to own property in Portugal to qualify for the D7 visa. You don't. A long-term rental contract is enough to meet the accommodation requirement.

That matters, because it means you don't have to rush a property decision under visa pressure. Rent first, get to know a neighbourhood through the seasons, then decide if buying makes sense.

Buying too early — before you actually know if a city suits your routine — is the single most common regret I hear from remote workers who moved to Portugal.

When Buying Actually Makes Sense

Once you've settled — usually after living in a city for six months to a year — buying starts to make financial sense if you plan to stay long-term or want a stable base rather than repeated rental hunts.

Lisbon and Porto remain the top choices for remote workers thanks to infrastructure, coworking spaces, and flight connectivity. The Algarve offers a slower pace with strong rental-yield potential if you travel often and want the property to earn while you're away.
